\chapter{Evaluation}%
\label{chp:evaluation}

\section{Evaluation Methodology}%
\label{sec:eval_methodology}

The evaluation assesses the pipeline against each of the objectives stated in Section~\ref{sec:intro_aims}. Four aspects are measured: the quality of the homography calibration, player detection coverage, end-to-end verdict accuracy, and the distribution of the offside margins produced.

\subsection{Ground Truth and Its Limitations}

The ground truth used for verdict evaluation is the offside annotation provided by the SoccerNet dataset~\cite{giancola2018soccernet,deliegev2soccernet}. Each clip in the dataset corresponds to an event at which the match linesman raised the offside flag; accordingly, the expected correct verdict for every event is \emph{offside}. This follows the approach taken by~\cite{panse2020dataset}, where the system was evaluated against manually verified annotations of broadcast images.

An important limitation of this ground truth is that SoccerNet annotations reflect match official judgement rather than an independent geometric measurement. Linesman errors are well-documented in marginal calls where the margin is within centimetres. Any discrepancy between system and annotation may therefore stem from system error or official error, and the two cannot be distinguished without independent measurement. Results are stratified by reported margin to address this: large-margin cases are unlikely to involve official error and provide a cleaner signal, while small-margin cases represent uncertainty for both the system and the linesman.

\subsection{Dataset}

A total of 457 offside clips were extracted from match footage covering different leagues including the English Premier League, Spanish La Liga, Italian Serie A, German Bundesliga, French Ligue 1, and UEFA Champions League. Of these, 293 were discarded at the keyframe annotation stage as unsuitable for the pipeline (replay shots, close-up angles, or frames in which the relevant players were not visible). The remaining 164 clips yielded annotated keyframes; 162 of these had sufficient visible pitch landmarks to produce a reliable homography, and 155 progressed through the offside checker to produce a verdict. A final quality filter excluded the 5 frames whose homography mean reprojection error exceeded 3.2\,px (more than three times the median calibration error), leaving \textbf{150 events} in the clean evaluation set. \section{Homography Reprojection Error}%
\label{sec:eval_homography}

The quality of each estimated homography is measured by the symmetric pixel reprojection error: the known world coordinates of each landmark correspondence are projected back into the image via $\mathbf{H}^{-1}$, and the Euclidean distance to the original clicked position is computed. A lower value indicates a more accurate calibration.

Across the 150 retained calibrated frames, the mean reprojection error was \textbf{1.00\,px} and the median was \textbf{0.92\,px}. 85.3\% of these frames were within the threshold of 2\,px and 14.7\% were above. To translate this pixel error into a world-space uncertainty, the local image-to-pitch scale of $\mathbf{H}^{-1}$ is evaluated at a representative point near the far touchline: one metre along the pitch surface projects to approximately 4--7 pixels of image displacement at this depth, so inverting the relationship maps one pixel of image error to approximately 0.14--0.25\,m of world-space displacement. The exact figure varies with depth because perspective compresses distant regions of the pitch more strongly than near regions. This figure sets a practical lower bound on the geometric precision of any offside measurement produced by the system: verdicts with margins below roughly 0.3\,m cannot be considered reliably resolved given the calibration precision.

\section{Player Detection}%
\label{sec:eval_detection}

YOLO11x-seg was applied to all 164 annotated keyframes. Across these frames, the mean number of players detected was \textbf{18.8} (minimum 8, maximum 36). No frame produced zero detections. The detection step runs prior to the homography quality filter, so detection statistics are reported across all 164 keyframes rather than the 150-event clean set. Visual inspection confirmed that the attacker and second-to-last defender were correctly detected in most frames. The main failure modes were peripheral detections (spectators, advertising staff) and occasional splitting of occluded players into overlapping boxes; neither affected final verdicts, as the operator interface allowed the correct attacker to be selected.

\section{End-to-End Offside Verdicts}%
\label{sec:eval_offside}

\subsection{Overall Agreement}

Of the 150 clean events, the system produced an \emph{offside} verdict for 128 (85.3\%) and an \emph{onside} verdict for 22 (14.7\%). Since SoccerNet annotates all events as offside, the 128 offside verdicts represent agreement with the ground truth annotation. The overall agreement rate is therefore \textbf{85.3\%}.

\subsection{Margin-Stratified Analysis}

The overall figure conflates cases of very different difficulty. Table~\ref{tab:margin_bands} disaggregates the results by the margin reported by the system, dividing events into three bands: clear cases (margin exceeding 1.0\,m), moderate cases (0.5--1.0\,m), and marginal cases (at most 0.5\,m).

The stratified results reveal a pattern consistent with the calibration precision characterised in Section~\ref{sec:eval_homography}. For events where the system measured a margin exceeding 1.0\,m, agreement with the SoccerNet annotation was 97.4\%; the single disagreement in this band is attributable to a documented linesman error rather than a system failure. For the moderate band (0.5--1.0\,m), agreement remained high at 86.8\%. For marginal events where the measured separation was at most 0.5\,m, agreement fell to 75.9\%, which is above the random-chance level of 50\% but reflects genuine geometric uncertainty: a reprojection error of approximately 1\,px translates to a world-space uncertainty of 15--25\,cm, meaning that for sub-0.5\,m events neither the system's measurement nor the match official's decision can be considered authoritative. Combining the clear and moderate bands gives a \emph{confident} set of 92 events for which the system's measurement substantially exceeds its estimated precision. On this subset, agreement with the SoccerNet annotation is \textbf{91.3\%}. This figure is the most informative summary of system accuracy, as it excludes the region where measurement uncertainty and potential official error are indistinguishable.

The median margin across all 150 clean events was \textbf{0.64\,m} and the mean was \textbf{0.79\,m}, with a range of 0.00--3.93\,m.

\subsection{Failure Modes}%
\label{sec:eval_failure}

Three categories of failure were identified.

\textbf{High-reprojection-error frames.} During development, a number of frames produced poorly conditioned homographies caused by near-singular landmark configurations --- that is, picked landmarks lying nearly collinear (so they fail to constrain rotation about the line) or concentrated in only one half of the visible pitch (so $\mathbf{H}$ extrapolates poorly to the opposite half) --- or by stewards and pitch-side personnel being incorrectly detected as outfield players and projecting outside the pitch boundary. These were addressed by a pitch-boundary filter in the offside checker (Section~\ref{sec:impl_challenges}) and the homography quality gate described in Section~\ref{sec:impl_homography}. The final quality filter excludes the 5 frames whose mean reprojection error exceeds 3.2\,px from the clean evaluation set.

\textbf{Team misidentification.} Three of the 22 onside verdicts in the clean set were attributable to team misidentification rather than geometric error: the attacker was assigned to the defending team's cluster, causing the system to evaluate the wrong player. This failure is most common when the two teams wear kits whose Lab colour vectors are close together, or when the visible torso area is small due to distance from the camera.

\textbf{Marginal measurement uncertainty.} The remaining 19 onside verdicts in the clean set occurred in the marginal or moderate bands (margin $\leq$ 1.0\,m). As discussed above, the one clear-band disagreement (margin 1.10\,m) is attributable to a documented linesman error rather than a system failure. The remaining cases fall within or close to the system's estimated measurement precision and cannot be conclusively attributed to system error.

Direct comparison is complicated by methodological differences: Panse and Mahabaleshwarkar use static images with manually provided role annotations, bypassing team identification and keyframe selection; Uchida \textit{et~al.}\ use two fixed calibrated cameras, providing a more accurate geometric reference at the cost of dedicated hardware. The present system achieves 91.3\% agreement on confident cases end-to-end from a single broadcast camera, exceeding the F1 of Uchida \textit{et~al.}\ (63.1\%) and matching Panse and Mahabaleshwarkar (85\%) while operating on video rather than pre-selected stills.
